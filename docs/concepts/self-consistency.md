# Self-consistency

**Does the same labeler give the same verdict twice?**

Measured by labeling the same items again later and comparing.

## Measured here: 50%

| Round | Result |
|---|---|
| 1 | 4 pass, 4 fail |
| 2 | 8 pass, 0 fail |
| Agreement | **4/8 = chance** |

On a binary task, 50% is a coin flip. The labels carried no information.

## Why it must come first

Judge-human agreement is uninterpretable if the human is unstable.
A judge cannot be evaluated against a labeler who flips a coin.

**Measure self-consistency before measuring agreement.**
This was done in the wrong order here.

## Consistency is not calibration

Round 3 was internally consistent — passed everything — and still wrong
on the same two cases. A labeler who always says PASS is perfectly
consistent and useless.
